# Greetings and introductions

Words and sentences for greeting people and introducing yourself. Some of these phrases were recorded from Kullilli speakers; others were made up by the guide's authors, based on how Kullilli and other Aboriginal languages work. Each one is marked.

## Saying hello
id: hello
grammar: Greetings & Introductions › Greetings, p. 18

The sources have few examples of greetings. Across Aboriginal languages there are generally two ways to greet people. One is to translate English greetings such as ‘good morning’. The other is found in languages where older greetings were recorded: phrases like ‘what news?’ or ‘where are you going?’, such as Bardi *anggi jawal* ‘what news’ and Yolŋu *nhämirr* ‘what-having’.

Something similar in Kullilli could be {mina yara-ani}, literally ‘what message’, a bit like English *what's up?*. This phrase is not recorded: the guide's authors made it up.

```ex made-up
mina yara-ani
what message
What news? (like ‘What's up?’)
```

Another way to greet someone is to ask where they are going. This sentence was recorded from Charlie Phillips:

```ex 181/53
ngala-anga ini yanhtha-gala
where-to   you go-present
Where are you going?
```

Q: What does {mina yara-ani} mean, word for word?
A: ‘what message’.

Q: Was {mina yara-ani} recorded from a Kullilli speaker?
A: No. The guide's authors made it up, based on greetings in other Aboriginal languages.

Q: How do you ask ‘Where are you going?’
A: {ngala-anga ini yanhtha-gala}.

## Saying goodbye
id: goodbye
grammar: Greetings & Introductions › Greetings, p. 18–19

To say goodbye, you could say ‘I'll see you soon’. This sentence was made up by the guide's authors:

```ex made-up
guntu ngathu nadya-rra  inha
soon  I      see-future you
I'll see you soon.
```

If you know when you'll see them, change {guntu} ‘soon’ to {wikarla} ‘tomorrow’ or {wikarlawatyu} ‘the day after tomorrow’.

This one is a real example from Charlie Phillips:

```ex 180/52
nganyi yanhtha-ba-rra
I      go-away-future
I'm going away.
```

For ‘good night’ you could say {dhali ngawu}, made from {dhali} ‘good’ and {ngawu} ‘night’. This isn't recorded as a phrase; the authors put the two words together.

Q: How would you say ‘I'll see you tomorrow’?
A: {wikarla ngathu nadya-rra inha}.

Q: What does {nganyi yanhtha-ba-rra} mean?
A: ‘I'm going away.’

Q: Which two words make up {dhali ngawu} ‘good night’?
A: {dhali} ‘good’ and {ngawu} ‘night’.

## Hey! gala and gari
id: hey
grammar: Greetings & Introductions › Greetings, p. 19

{gala} and {gari} mean something like ‘hey’, and might be used to catch someone's attention. (In some languages, like Bardi, a word like this is used more to wrap up a conversation or a story.)

```ex
gala yurra
hey  you.all
Hey, you all.
```

```ex
gari walya yanhtha / nadya-rra  ngathu inha
hey  not   go      / see-future I      you
Hey, don't go away, I want to see you.
```

Q: Give two Kullilli words for ‘hey’.
A: {gala} and {gari}.

Q: How do you say ‘Hey, you all’?
A: {gala yurra}.

Q: In the second example, which word means ‘not’?
A: {walya}.

## What's your name?
id: names
grammar: Greetings & Introductions › Introductions, p. 19

{yarinya} means ‘name’. To ask someone their name:

```ex 332/12
wani ingka yarinya-ani
what your name
What's your name?
```

To answer, or to introduce yourself first, put your name in the gap, with {-nha} after it:

```ex
nganytya yarinya-ani     ____-nha
my       name-nominative ____
My name is ____.
```

Q: What is the Kullilli word for ‘name’?
A: {yarinya}.

Q: How do you ask someone their name?
A: {wani ingka yarinya-ani}.

Q: How would someone called Toby say ‘My name is Toby’?
A: {nganytya yarinya-ani Toby-nha}.

## Where are you from?
id: where-from
grammar: Greetings & Introductions › Introductions, p. 19–20

When you meet someone, you might ask where they are from:

```ex 133/5
ngala-andru ini
where-from  you
Where are you from?
```

Kullilli has several words for ‘you’: one for one person ({ini}), one for two people ({yula}), and one for three or more ({yurra}). (There's more about this in the Pronouns unit.)

```ex
ngala-andru yula
where-from  you.two
Where are you two from?
```

```ex
ngala-andru yurra
where-from  you.all
Where are you all from?
```

Q: Which word for ‘you’ do you use when talking to two people?
A: {yula}.

Q: How do you ask a group of five people where they are from?
A: {ngala-andru yurra}.

Q: What does {-andru} mean in {ngala-andru}?
A: ‘from’.

## I'm from…
id: im-from
grammar: Greetings & Introductions › Introductions, p. 20

To say where you are from, put {-andru} ‘from’ on the place:

```ex 134/6
ngaka-nuga-andru
water-big-from
(I'm, we're) from the big water.
```

```ex
____-andru
____-from
(I'm, we're) from ____.
```

You can use any place name, such as {Brisbane-andru} ‘from Brisbane’ or {Dynevor-andru} ‘from Dynevor’.

Q: How do you say ‘I'm from Brisbane’?
A: {Brisbane-andru}.

Q: What does {ngaka-nuga-andru} mean?
A: ‘from the big water’.

Q: Where does {-andru} go: before or after the place name?
A: After it, joined on as an ending.

## Do you speak Kullilli?
id: speak-kullilli
grammar: Greetings & Introductions › Introductions, p. 20

One more question to practise with other learners, with two answers to choose from:

```ex
yanhdha-gala  ini garlala-nha
speak-present you Kullilli
Do you speak Kullilli?
```

```ex
ga  waywa nganyi yanhdha-gala
yes small I      speak-present
Yes, I speak a little.
```

```ex
dhali nganyi yanhdha-gala
good  I      speak-present
I speak well.
```

Now you can greet people, and ask and answer three questions when you meet someone for the first time.

Q: What is the Kullilli for ‘yes’?
A: {ga}.

Q: How do you say ‘I speak well’?
A: {dhali nganyi yanhdha-gala}.

Q: Which word in the question means ‘Kullilli’?
A: {garlala-nha}.
