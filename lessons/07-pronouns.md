# Pronouns: I, you, they

Pronouns are words that stand for a person, animal, place or thing. Kullilli pronouns change with **person** (I, you, or someone else), **number** (one, two, or more) and **case** (the job the pronoun does, as with the noun endings).

## I and me: nganyi, ngathu, nganha
id: i-and-me
grammar: Pronouns › Subjects and Objects – Singular, p. 41–42

English has *I* for the doer or feeler (the *subject*: *I saw the dog*, *I am hungry*) and *me* for the one something is done to (the *object*: *The dog saw me*). Kullilli has this difference too, but it has **two** words for ‘I’.

{nganyi} ‘I’ is used with verbs that have no object, and with verbs ending in {-iyi} (doing something to yourself). This is the *nominative* ‘I’.

```ex 180/52
nganyi  yanhtha-ba-rra
1sg.nom go-away-future
I am going away.
```

```ex 87/23
galka-iyi-gali         nganyi
strike-reflexive-past 1sg.nom
I hit myself.
```

{ngathu} ‘I’ is used with verbs that have an object. This is the *ergative* ‘I’.

```ex 346/26
dhupu-anha       ngathu  wupi-gala
smoke-accusative 1sg.erg smell-present
I smell smoke.
```

{nganha} ‘me’ is the object, the *accusative*.

```ex 314/58
gala-ba     nganha
answer-away 1sg.acc
Answer me.
```

| Subject (nominative) | Subject (ergative) | Object (accusative) |
|---|---|---|
| {nganyi} ‘I’ | {ngathu} ‘I’ | {nganha} ‘me’ |

Q: Which ‘I’ do you use with a verb that has an object?
A: {ngathu}.

Q: What is the Kullilli for ‘me’?
A: {nganha}.

Q: Which ‘I’ is used in ‘I am going away’, and why?
A: {nganyi}, because the verb has no object.

## You (one person): ini, yundru, inha
id: you-one
grammar: Pronouns › Subjects and Objects – Singular, p. 42–43

English has one word, *you*, for all of these jobs. Kullilli has three words for ‘you’ (talking to one person):

```ex 459/11
ini     yanhtha-ba
2sg.nom go-away
You go on.
```

```ex 380/60
yundru  gapa-rra      nganha
2sg.erg follow-future 1sg.acc
You will follow me.
```

```ex 220/28
mura ngathu  inha    bura-nga
OK   1sg.erg 2sg.acc tell-past
OK, I told you.
```

| Subject (nominative) | Subject (ergative) | Object (accusative) |
|---|---|---|
| {ini} ‘you’ | {yundru} ‘you’ | {inha} ‘you’ |

Q: Which ‘you’ is the object?
A: {inha}.

Q: Which ‘you’ is used in ‘You will follow me’, and why?
A: {yundru}, because ‘follow’ has an object ({nganha} ‘me’).

Q: How many words for ‘you’ (one person) have you met in this lesson?
A: Three: {ini}, {yundru} and {inha}.

## I ask you, you ask me
id: i-ask-you
grammar: Pronouns › Subjects and Objects – Singular, p. 43

Now we can put ‘I’ and ‘you’ together. The verb {gunka} ‘ask’ has an object, so the subject must be ergative, not nominative.

```ex 310/54
gunka-gala  ngathu  inha
ask-present 1sg.erg 2sg.acc
I ask you.
```

The other way round:

```ex made-up
gunka-gala  yundru  nganha
ask-present 2sg.erg 1sg.acc
You ask me.
```

Q: How do you say ‘You ask me’?
A: {gunka-gala yundru nganha}.

Q: Why is ‘I’ {ngathu} and not {nganyi} in ‘I ask you’?
A: Because ‘ask’ has an object, so the subject is ergative.

Q: Which word is the object in {gunka-gala ngathu inha}?
A: {inha} ‘you’.

## He, she, it: nhani, nhandru, nhanha
id: he-she-it
grammar: Pronouns › Subjects and Objects – Singular, p. 43–44

English uses *he* and *she* for people and *it* for things. Kullilli doesn't make these differences: one set of words is used for any person or thing, male or female, living or not. You just need the right subject or object form.

```ex 16
nhani   walya gari-gala
3sg.nom not   burn-present
It isn't burning.
```

```ex 280/24
girni-anha        nhandru-guru  gatyi-li-rra
strong-accusative 3sg.erg-there break-apart-future
He will break apart the strong (thing).
```

```ex 17
wirnbi-dhika nhanha-guru
throw-back   3sg.acc-there
Throw it away.
```

| Subject (nominative) | Subject (ergative) | Object (accusative) |
|---|---|---|
| {nhani} ‘he, she, it, they (one person)’ | {nhandru} ‘he, she, it, they’ | {nhanha} ‘him, her, it, them’ |

Q: Does Kullilli have different words for ‘he’ and ‘she’?
A: No. {nhani} (and {nhandru}, {nhanha}) is used for any person or thing.

Q: Which form is used in ‘Throw it away’, and why?
A: {nhanha}, because ‘it’ is the object.

Q: What does {nhani walya gari-gala} mean?
A: ‘It isn't burning.’

## Words just for men: nhina, nhulu, nhinha
id: masculine-pronouns
grammar: Pronouns › Subjects and Objects – Singular, p. 44–45

Kullilli also has third-person pronouns that refer only to males: {nhia} or {nhina} (nominative), {nhulu} (ergative) and {nhinha} (object). The sources suggest they weren't used very often, and {nhani}, {nhandru} and {nhanha} can refer to males too. Perhaps they were going out of use, the way *thee* and *thou* have in English.

```ex 327/7
mina-anga   nhina        dhithi-gala
what-dative 3sg.masc.nom laugh-present
Why (is he) laughing?
```

```ex 313/57
walya nganha  nhulu        gala-ba-nga
not   1sg.acc 3sg.masc.erg answer-away-past
He didn't answer me.
```

```ex
gunka-nga ngathu  nhinha
ask-past  1sg.erg 3sg.masc.acc
I asked him.
```

| Subject (nominative) | Subject (ergative) | Object (accusative) |
|---|---|---|
| {nhia}, {nhina} ‘he’ | {nhulu} ‘he’ | {nhinha} ‘him’ |

Q: Which of these pronouns is the object form?
A: {nhinha} ‘him’.

Q: Do you have to use these words when talking about a man?
A: No. {nhani}, {nhandru} and {nhanha} can refer to men too.

Q: What does {gunka-nga ngathu nhinha} mean?
A: ‘I asked him.’

## Patterns in the singular pronouns
id: singular-patterns
grammar: Pronouns › Subjects and Objects – Singular, p. 45

Here are the nine main singular subject and object pronouns:

| Person | Subject (nominative) | Subject (ergative) | Object (accusative) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st (I, me) | {nga-nyi} | {nga-thu} | {nga-nha} |
| 2nd (you) | {i-ni} | {yu-ndru} | {i-nha} |
| 3rd (he, she, it) | {nha-ni} | {nha-ndru} | {nha-nha} |

Some patterns help you remember them:

- All the first-person forms start with {nga-}, and all the third-person forms start with {nha-}.
- The second-person forms start with {i-}, except the ergative {yundru}.
- The second and third person have the same endings: {-ni} (nominative), {-ndru} (ergative), {-nha} (accusative). The first-person endings are similar but not quite the same.

Linguists call sets of endings like these *declensions*.

Q: What do all the third-person singular pronouns start with?
A: {nha-}.

Q: Which second-person form breaks the {i-} pattern?
A: {yundru}.

Q: Which ending do all the singular object (accusative) pronouns share?
A: {-nha}.

## The two of us: ngali
id: we-two
grammar: Pronouns › Subjects and Objects – Dual, p. 46

English has *we*, *you* and *they* for more than one person. Kullilli also has special **dual** pronouns for exactly **two** people or things: ‘the two of us’, ‘the two of you’, ‘the two of them’. For pairs, you use these instead of the plural pronouns.

```ex 43
murta-iyi-gala          ngali
hurt-reflexive-present 1dl.nom
The two of us feel sick.
```

```ex 151/23
wiyi-nha        bari  ngali-ngu    gulpa-rra
fire-accusative think 1dl-ergative light-future
I think we (the two of us) will light a fire.
```

```ex 281/25
ngutya-rra      ngali-nha
give-imperative 1dl-accusative
Give to the two of us.
```

| Subject (nominative) | Subject (ergative) | Object (accusative) |
|---|---|---|
| {ngali} | {ngali-ngu} | {ngali-nha} |

Q: What does *dual* mean?
A: Exactly two.

Q: How do you say ‘the two of us’ as the object?
A: {ngali-nha}.

Q: Which form is used in 151/23, where the two of us light a fire?
A: {ngali-ngu}, the ergative, because ‘light’ has an object (the fire).

## The two of you: yula
id: you-two
grammar: Pronouns › Subjects and Objects – Dual, p. 46–47

```ex 241/49
yula    dhina-ba-rra
2dl.nom stand.up-away-future
The two of you stand up.
```

```ex 74/10
yula-ngu     nguthi nuga nganha  ngutya-rra
2dl-ergative meat   big  1sg.acc give-future
The two of you will give me much meat.
```

```ex 71/7
ngathu  yula-nha       ngutya-gala
1sg.erg 2dl-accusative give-present
I give it to you (the two of you).
```

| Subject (nominative) | Subject (ergative) | Object (accusative) |
|---|---|---|
| {yula} | {yula-ngu} | {yula-nha} |

Q: What is the ergative of {yula}?
A: {yula-ngu}.

Q: What does {yula dhina-ba-rra} mean?
A: ‘The two of you stand up.’

Q: How do you say ‘the two of you’ as the object?
A: {yula-nha}.

## The two of them: bula
id: they-two
grammar: Pronouns › Subjects and Objects – Dual, p. 47–48

```ex 81/17
bula-ba           guri
3dl.nom-emphatic this.way
The two of them (are coming) this way.
```

```ex 4
murra    bula-guru-ngu      muraba-nga  gunga-anha
finished 3dl-there-ergative finish-past food-accusative
OK, the two of them have finished the food.
```

```ex 285/29
nguta-la-rra-ba           bula-nha
give-?-imperative-emphatic 3dl-accusative
Give to the two of them.
```

{-bula} can also go on a noun: {garna} ‘man’ becomes {garna-bula} ‘two men’. It can go on a describing word too:

```ex 215/23
garna-bula garlgugarlgu-bula
man-dual   weak.weak-dual
The two men are weak.
```

| Subject (nominative) | Subject (ergative) | Object (accusative) |
|---|---|---|
| {bula} | {bula-ngu} | {bula-nha} |

(A question mark in the English line means we don't know what that part means.)

Q: How do you say ‘two men’?
A: {garna-bula}.

Q: What is the object form of {bula}?
A: {bula-nha}.

Q: In 215/23, which two words have {-bula}?
A: {garna-bula} ‘two men’ and {garlgugarlgu-bula} ‘weak (two)’.

## The dual pattern
id: dual-pattern
grammar: Pronouns › Subjects and Objects – Dual, p. 48

The dual pronouns are simpler than the singular ones. Start with the nominative, add {-ngu} for the ergative and {-nha} for the accusative. There are no irregular forms.

| Person | Subject (nominative) | Subject (ergative) | Object (accusative) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st (the two of us) | {ngali} | {ngali-ngu} | {ngali-nha} |
| 2nd (the two of you) | {yula} | {yula-ngu} | {yula-nha} |
| 3rd (the two of them) | {bula} | {bula-ngu} | {bula-nha} |

Q: How do you make the ergative of a dual pronoun?
A: Add {-ngu} to the nominative.

Q: How do you make the accusative of a dual pronoun?
A: Add {-nha} to the nominative.

Q: Are there any irregular dual pronouns?
A: No.

## We all: ngandra
id: we-all
grammar: Pronouns › Subjects and Objects – Plural, p. 48–49

*Plural* pronouns are for **three or more**. In English: *we*, *you all* (*youse*, *you guys*, *you lot*) and *they*.

```ex 32
ngaymari-nga ngandra
thirsty-past 1pl.nom
We are thirsty.
```

```ex 466/18
nanpa-rra   ngandra-ngu  nhinha
bury-future 1pl-ergative 3sg.masc.acc
We will bury him.
```

```ex 283/27
ngutya-dhika-ri       ngandra-nha
give-back-irrealis 1pl-accusative
(You might) give back to us all.
```

| Subject (nominative) | Subject (ergative) | Object (accusative) |
|---|---|---|
| {ngandra} | {ngandra-ngu} | {ngandra-nha} |

Q: How many people do plural pronouns refer to?
A: Three or more.

Q: How do you say ‘we’ (all of us) as the subject of a verb with an object?
A: {ngandra-ngu}.

Q: What does {ngaymari-nga ngandra} mean?
A: ‘We are thirsty.’

## You all: yurra
id: you-all
grammar: Pronouns › Subjects and Objects – Plural, p. 49

```ex 5
yurra   muruba-nga
2pl.nom go-past
You all are finished.
lit: You all have gone.
```

```ex 72/8
yurra-ngu    dhalhtha-rra
2pl-ergative eat-purpose
You (all) eat meat.
```

```ex 72/8
ngathu  nguthi-anha     yurra-nha      ngutya-gala
1sg.erg meat-accusative 2pl-accusative give-present
I give meat to you (all) to eat.
```

| Subject (nominative) | Subject (ergative) | Object (accusative) |
|---|---|---|
| {yurra} | {yurra-ngu} | {yurra-nha} |

Q: How do you say ‘you all’ as the object?
A: {yurra-nha}.

Q: What is the nominative ‘you all’?
A: {yurra}.

Q: In the last example, who gives the meat?
A: ‘I’ ({ngathu}).

## They all: dhana
id: they-all
grammar: Pronouns › Subjects and Objects – Plural, p. 50

```ex 390/6
dhuna-wira gurnrtu-nu dhana-guru bunhthi-na-rra
time-wait  soon-?     3pl-there  come-?-future
Wait a while, soon they will all come past.
```

```ex 1
dhana-guru-ngu     muraba-nga  gunga-anha
3pl-there-ergative finish-past food-accusative
All of them have finished this food.
```

```ex 286/30
ngutya-la-rra-ba      dhana-nha
give-?-future-emphatic 3pl-accusative
Give to them all.
```

| Subject (nominative) | Subject (ergative) | Object (accusative) |
|---|---|---|
| {dhana} | {dhana-ngu} | {dhana-nha} |

Just like the dual, every plural pronoun adds {-ngu} for the ergative and {-nha} for the accusative:

| Person | Subject (nominative) | Subject (ergative) | Object (accusative) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st (we all) | {ngandra} | {ngandra-ngu} | {ngandra-nha} |
| 2nd (you all) | {yurra} | {yurra-ngu} | {yurra-nha} |
| 3rd (they all) | {dhana} | {dhana-ngu} | {dhana-nha} |

Q: What is the ergative of {dhana}?
A: {dhana-ngu}.

Q: How do you say ‘Give to them all’?
A: {ngutya-la-rra-ba dhana-nha}.

Q: Do the plural pronouns follow the same pattern as the dual?
A: Yes: {-ngu} for the ergative and {-nha} for the accusative.

## Showing plural with buka
id: plural-buka
grammar: Pronouns › Another way to show plural nouns, p. 50–51

Another way to show that a noun is plural is the word {buka} ‘all’. You wouldn't say ‘all’ about one or two things, so if {buka} is there, the noun must mean three or more, even without a plural pronoun nearby.

```ex 214/22
garna-ani      dhana-guru garlgugarlgu-ani     buka
man-nominative 3pl-there  weak.weak-nominative all
The men are all weak.
```

The sentence only says {garna} ‘man’, but because {buka} is there we know it means more than two men. You can also use {wandru} ‘many, a lot’ to talk about plural nouns.

Q: What does {buka} tell you about a noun?
A: That it is plural (three or more).

Q: In 214/22, how do we know it means ‘men’ and not ‘man’?
A: Because of {buka} ‘all’ (and the plural pronoun {dhana-guru}).

Q: Which other word can show that there are many?
A: {wandru} ‘many, a lot’.
