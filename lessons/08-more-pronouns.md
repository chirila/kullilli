# Pronouns: my, to me, from me

Pronouns can take the other case endings too: the dative, locative and ablative. With pronouns the meanings can be a little different.

## My, your, his: possessive pronouns
id: possessive-pronouns
grammar: Pronouns › Dative Pronouns, p. 51–52

With pronouns, the dative means **possession**, like English *my, mine, our, your, his, her, their*.

| Person | Singular | Dual | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | {nga-nytya} ‘my’ | {ngala-ngani} ‘our (two)’ | {ngandra-ngani} ‘our (all)’ |
| 2nd | {i-ngka} ‘your’ | {yula-ngani} ‘your (two)’ | {yurra-ngani} ‘your (all)’ |
| 3rd | {nhu-ngka} ‘his, her, its’ | {bula-ngani} ‘their (two)’ | {dhana-ngani} ‘their (all)’ |

The dual and plural forms all end in {-ngani}. The second- and third-person singular end in {-ngka}, and ‘my’ is {nganytya}.

```words
nganytya ngarnidya | my father
ingka dhithi | your dog
```

There is much more on possession in the unit on owning and belonging.

Q: How do you say ‘your’ (talking to one person)?
A: {ingka}.

Q: Which ending do all the dual and plural possessive pronouns have?
A: {-ngani}.

Q: How do you say ‘our’ (three or more of us)?
A: {ngandra-ngani}.

## To me, to you: -ngala
id: locative-pronouns
grammar: Pronouns › Locative Pronouns, p. 52–54

With pronouns, the locative shows the **goal** of an action: who or what it goes towards, like *me* in *Speak to me*. English uses *me* for both ‘me’ the object and ‘to me’, but Kullilli has different words. These pronouns end in {-ngala}, except ‘to me’, which is {nganala}.

```ex 247/55
yanhdha-rra      nganala
speak-imperative 1sg.loc
Speak to me.
```

```ex 249/57
yanhdha ngalangala
speak   1dl.loc
Speak to the two of us.
```

```ex 253/61
gawa ngandra yanhdha-rra  nhungala-burlu
come 1pl.nom speak-future 3sg.loc-over.there
Come on, we will talk to that one over there.
```

| Person | Singular | Dual | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | {nga-nala} | {ngala-ngala} | {ngandra-ngala} |
| 2nd | {i-ngala} | {yula-ngala}* | {yurra-ngala} |
| 3rd | {nhu-ngala} | {bula-ngala}* | {dhana-ngala}* |

* These forms aren't found in the sources. They have been worked out from the regular pattern.

Q: How do you say ‘Speak to me’?
A: {yanhdha-rra nganala}.

Q: What ending do most of these pronouns have?
A: {-ngala}.

Q: What does a star (*) in the table mean?
A: The form isn't found in the sources; it was worked out from the pattern.

## Away from me: -ngura
id: ablative-pronouns
grammar: Pronouns › Ablative Pronouns, p. 54

Like ablative nouns, ablative pronouns show what an action moves **away from**. They end in {-ngura}, except ‘from me’, which is {nganura}.

```ex 453/5
yanhtha-ba nganura
go-away    1sg.abl
Go away from me.
```

| Person | Singular | Dual | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | {nga-nura} | {ngala-ngura} | {ngandra-ngura} |
| 2nd | {i-ngura} | {yula-ngura} | {yurra-ngura} |
| 3rd | {nhu-ngura} | {bula-ngura}* | {dhana-ngura}* |

* Not found in the sources; worked out from the pattern.

Q: How do you say ‘Go away from me’?
A: {yanhtha-ba nganura}.

Q: What is ‘away from you all’?
A: {yurra-ngura}.

Q: Which form breaks the {-ngura} pattern?
A: {nganura} ‘from me’.

## How the pronouns fit together
id: pronoun-patterns
grammar: Pronouns › Summary on Kullilli personal pronouns, p. 54–55

Which pronoun you use depends on three things:

- **person**: first, second or third
- **number**: singular, dual or plural
- **case**: nominative, ergative, accusative, dative, locative or ablative

That makes 54 pronouns (more with the masculine ones), but there are patterns. Most pronouns are a root plus a case ending. These are the roots (where there are two, which one is used depends on the case):

| Person | Singular | Dual | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | {nga-} | {ngali-}, {ngala-} | {ngandra-} |
| 2nd | {i-}, {yu-} | {yula-} | {yurra-} |
| 3rd | {nha-}, {nhu-} | {bula-} | {dhana-} |

The endings come in three sets: one for ‘I’, one for ‘you’ and ‘he, she, it’, and one for all the dual and plural pronouns.

| | Nominative | Ergative | Accusative | Dative | Locative | Ablative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st person singular | {-nyi} | {-thu} | {-nha} | {-nytya} | {-nala} | {-nura} |
| 2nd and 3rd singular | {-ni} | {-ndru} | {-nha} | {-ngka} | {-ngala} | {-ngura} |
| all dual and plural | (none) | {-ngu} | {-nha} | {-ngani} | {-ngala} | {-ngura} |

Q: What three things decide which pronoun to use?
A: Person, number and case.

Q: Using the tables, what is the locative of {dhana}?
A: {dhana-ngala}.

Q: Which ending is the same in all three sets?
A: The accusative {-nha} (the locative and ablative are also nearly the same).

## All the pronouns
id: pronoun-table
grammar: Pronouns › Summary on Kullilli personal pronouns, p. 56

Here are all 54 personal pronouns in one table, for reference.

| | Nominative | Ergative | Accusative | Dative | Locative | Ablative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | {nga-nyi} | {nga-thu} | {nga-nha} | {nga-nytya} | {nga-nala} | {nga-nura} |
| you (one) | {i-ni} | {yu-ndru} | {i-nha} | {i-ngka} | {i-ngala} | {i-ngura} |
| he, she, it | {nha-ni} | {nha-ndru} | {nha-nha} | {nhu-ngka} | {nhu-ngala} | {nhu-ngura} |
| we two | {ngali} | {ngali-ngu} | {ngali-nha} | {ngala-ngani} | {ngala-ngala} | {ngala-ngura} |
| you two | {yula} | {yula-ngu} | {yula-nha} | {yula-ngani} | {yula-ngala} | {yula-ngura} |
| they two | {bula} | {bula-ngu} | {bula-nha} | {bula-ngani} | {bula-ngala} | {bula-ngura} |
| we all | {ngandra} | {ngandra-ngu} | {ngandra-nha} | {ngandra-ngani} | {ngandra-ngala} | {ngandra-ngura} |
| you all | {yurra} | {yurra-ngu} | {yurra-nha} | {yurra-ngani} | {yurra-ngala} | {yurra-ngura} |
| they all | {dhana} | {dhana-ngu} | {dhana-nha} | {dhana-ngani} | {dhana-ngala} | {dhana-ngura} |

The forms with irregular roots are in the singular rows ({yu-} in {yundru}, {nhu-} in {nhungka}) and the dual ‘we two’ row ({ngala-} in the dative, locative and ablative).

Q: What is the dative of ‘you two’?
A: {yula-ngani}.

Q: Which root does ‘he, she, it’ use in the dative, locative and ablative?
A: {nhu-} ({nhungka}, {nhungala}, {nhungura}).

Q: What is the ergative of ‘we all’?
A: {ngandra-ngu}.
