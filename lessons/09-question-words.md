# Question words

Words for asking *who?*, *what?*, *why?* and *where?*. Like nouns and pronouns, they take case endings that depend on their job in the sentence.

## Who? wara-ni, wara-lu
id: who
grammar: Pronouns › Question Pronouns › Human interrogative pronouns (“who”), p. 56–58

The word for ‘who’ is {wara}, and it takes different endings, just as English has *who*, *whom* and *whose*.

| Case | Form |
|---|---|
| nominative | {wara-ni} |
| ergative | {wara-lu} |
| accusative | {wara-nha} |
| dative | {wara-nga} |

‘Who ran away?’ has a doer but nothing that is done to, so ‘who’ is nominative:

```ex made-up
wara-ni         nhani-guru    guthi-nga
who-nominative 3sg.nom-there run-past
Who ran away?
```

Picking something up is done **to** something (the stick), so ‘who’ is ergative:

```ex 111/47
wara-lu      nganytya manhdha-nga nhangala makurra-nha
who-ergative 1sg.dat  grasp-past  3sg.loc  stick-accusative
Who picked up my stick here?
```

Q: What is the base word for ‘who’?
A: {wara}.

Q: Which ‘who’ is used in ‘Who picked up my stick?’, and why?
A: {wara-lu}, the ergative, because picking up is done to something (the stick).

Q: How do you say ‘Who ran away?’
A: {wara-ni nhani-guru guthi-nga}.

## Who is that? Whose?
id: whom-whose
grammar: Pronouns › Question Pronouns › Human interrogative pronouns (“who”), p. 58

The accusative {wara-nha} is used in questions like ‘Who is that man?’. Think of the answer, *That man is ____*: the blank is a bit like the object of *is*, so ‘who’ takes the accusative.

```ex 335/15
wara-nha       nhia-guru          garni-ya
who-accusative 3sg.masc.nom-there man-there(?)
Who is that man?
```

The dative {wara-nga} means ‘whose’, asking who something belongs to:

```ex 94/30
wara-nga   nhani   dhithi-ani     guthi-dhika-dhika-gala
who-dative 3sg.nom dog-nominative run-go.back-go.back-present
Whose is this dog running around?
```

Q: How do you say ‘whose’?
A: {wara-nga}.

Q: Which form of ‘who’ is used in ‘Who is that man?’
A: The accusative, {wara-nha}.

Q: What does {wara-nga nhani dhithi-ani…} ask?
A: Whose dog this is.

## What? minha
id: what
grammar: Pronouns › Question Pronouns › Non-human interrogative pronouns (“what”), p. 58–59

The base word for ‘what’ is {mina}. For an ordinary ‘what’ question, Kullilli uses the accusative, {minha} ({mina} with {-nha}):

```ex 489/41
minha yarinya-ni      nhani-yi     marni-ani
what  name-nominative 3sg.nom-here ceremonial.dance-nominative
What is the name of this dance?
```

The answer would be *The name of this dance is ____*, and as with ‘who’, the blank is a bit like the object of *is*.

There is also {wani} ‘what’, but it has only been found in one phrase, ‘What's your name?’:

```ex 332/12
wani ingka   yarinya-ani
what 2sg.dat name-nominative
What's your name?
```

Q: What is the usual word for ‘what?’
A: {minha}.

Q: Which two parts make up {minha}?
A: {mina} ‘what’ and the accusative ending {-nha}.

Q: Where is {wani} used?
A: Only in the question ‘What's your name?’ ({wani ingka yarinya-ani}).

## Why? mina-anga
id: why
grammar: Pronouns › Question Pronouns › Non-human interrogative pronouns (“what”), p. 59

‘Why’ is {mina} ‘what’ with the dative ending {-anga}: literally ‘what for?’. The dative is used for the receiver or purpose of an action; here the ‘purpose’ is the reason, so {mina-anga} means ‘for what reason?’

```ex 327/7
mina-anga   nhina        dhithi-gala
what-dative 3sg.masc.nom laugh-present
Why (is he) laughing?
```

Q: How do you say ‘why?’
A: {mina-anga}.

Q: What does {mina-anga} mean literally?
A: ‘what for?’

Q: Which ending turns {mina} ‘what’ into ‘why’?
A: The dative ending {-anga}.

## Where? Where from? Where to?
id: where
grammar: Pronouns › Question Pronouns › Place interrogative pronouns (“where”), p. 59–61

The base word for ‘where’ is {ngala}.

| Case | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| nominative | {ngala-ani} | where? |
| ablative | {ngala-andru} | where from? |
| dative | {ngala-anga} | where to? |

```ex 10
ngala-ani        ingka   ngurra
where-nominative 2sg.dat camp
Where is your camp?
```

```ex 133/5
ngala-andru    ini
where-ablative 2sg.nom
Where are you from?
```

```ex 181/53
ngala-anga   ini     yanhtha-gala
where-dative 2sg.nom go-present
Where are you going?
```

These are useful when you meet someone for the first time (see Greetings).

Q: How do you ask ‘Where is your camp?’
A: {ngala-ani ingka ngurra}.

Q: Which ending makes ‘where to?’
A: {-anga} (dative): {ngala-anga}.

Q: Why does ‘where from?’ use the ablative?
A: Because the ablative is about movement away from somewhere.
