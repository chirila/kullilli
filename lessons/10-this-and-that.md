# This and that

How to point things out: *this boomerang*, *that meat*. Words like these are called *demonstratives*.

## This: -yi
id: this-yi
grammar: This and that, p. 62

In English you might point at a book near you and say *this book*, and at one further away and say *that book*. Kullilli does this with the endings {-yi} ‘this’ and {-guru} ‘that’. {-yi} also means ‘here’, and {-guru} also means ‘there’.

{-yi} attaches to the pronouns you have already met, such as {nhani} and {nhanha}:

```ex 336/16
bakaranyi-ani       nhani-yi     gatyi-gali-ani
boomerang-nominative 3sg.nom-here break-past-nominative
This boomerang is broken.
```

```ex 169/41
yandra-anha     nhanha-yi    warlpa-ba-rra        ngathu
rock-accusative 3sg.acc-here rise-emphatic-future 1sg.erg
I lift this stone.
```

```ex 230/38
malyu warlpa-gala  nhani-yi     garna-ani      nganytya
anger rise-present 3sg.nom-here man-nominative 1sg.dat
This man is angry with me.
```

Q: Which ending means ‘this’?
A: {-yi}.

Q: What does {-yi} attach to in these examples?
A: To pronouns, such as {nhani} and {nhanha}.

Q: What does {bakaranyi-ani nhani-yi gatyi-gali-ani} mean?
A: ‘This boomerang is broken.’

## That: -guru
id: that-guru
grammar: This and that, p. 62–63

To say ‘that’, attach {-guru}:

```ex 343/23
yamba-nyara-gala          walga-ni         buka dhana-guru
fight-reciprocal-present woman-nominative all  3pl-there
That group of women are having a fight.
```

```ex 350/30
bunma   nhanha-guru   nguthi-anha
uncover 3sg.acc-there meat-accusative
Uncover that meat.
```

```ex 353/33
mura yundru  murlpa-nga nhanha-guru   bakaranyi
OK   2sg.erg make-past  3sg.acc-there boomerang
You made that boomerang.
```

McDonald and Wurm translated {-yi} as ‘here’ and {-guru} as ‘there’. The guide's authors think ‘this’ and ‘that’ fit them better.

Q: Which ending means ‘that’?
A: {-guru}.

Q: How do you say ‘Uncover that meat’?
A: {bunma nhanha-guru nguthi-anha}.

Q: How did McDonald and Wurm translate {-yi} and {-guru}?
A: As ‘here’ and ‘there’.

## Matching the pronoun to the noun
id: demonstrative-agreement
grammar: This and that, p. 63

When a pronoun with {-guru} or {-yi} goes with a noun, the pronoun has the **same case** as the noun. Linguists call this *agreement*.

In ‘Uncover that meat’, the meat has something done to it, so {nguthi} ‘meat’ has the accusative {-anha}, and the pronoun is the accusative {nhanha} (‘it’), with {-guru} attached:

```ex 350/30
bunma   nhanha-guru   nguthi-anha
uncover 3sg.acc-there meat-accusative
Uncover that meat.
```

Sentence 353/33 (in the last lesson) is a bit different: {nhanha-guru} is accusative, but {bakaranyi} ‘boomerang’ has no ending.

Q: In ‘Uncover that meat’, which two words are accusative?
A: {nhanha-guru} and {nguthi-anha}.

Q: What do linguists call this matching of cases?
A: Agreement.

Q: Which pronoun would go with a nominative noun ‘that dog’: {nhani-guru} or {nhanha-guru}?
A: {nhani-guru}, because {nhani} is the nominative.
