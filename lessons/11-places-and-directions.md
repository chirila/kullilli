# Places and directions

Saying where things are (over there, far away, close by) and which way actions go.

## Where it happens: -lunga, -langa
id: place-locative
grammar: Locations and directions › Locations, p. 64; The locative on nouns, p. 67

Remember the locative endings {-lunga} and {-langa}, like English *in*, *at* or *on*. On a noun, they show the place where something happens.

```ex 497/49
buli-gali nganyi  / mara galka-iyi-gali         nganyi  nhungala-guru makurra-lunga
fall-past 1sg.nom / hand strike-reflexive-past 1sg.nom 3sg.loc-there tree-locative
I fell and hit my hand on this stick.
```

The stick (literally ‘tree’, {makurra}) is where the hand was hit, so {makurra} takes {-lunga}.

```ex 161/33
gulha-rra      ini     nganytya ngurra-langa
sit-imperative 2sg.nom 1sg.dat  camp-locative
Sit down in my camp.
```

The camp is where the sitting should happen.

Q: What do {-lunga} and {-langa} show on a noun?
A: The place where something happens (‘in, at, on’).

Q: In 497/49, where was the hand hit?
A: On the stick: {makurra-lunga}.

Q: How do you say ‘in my camp’?
A: {nganytya ngurra-langa}.

## Over there: -burlu
id: burlu
grammar: Locations and directions › Locations, p. 65

The ending {-burlu} goes on pronouns and means ‘down there, over there’. Here it is on {nhungala} (‘to him, her, it’), making ‘that one over there’:

```ex 253/61
gawa ngandra yanhdha-rra  nhungala-burlu
come 1pl.nom speak-future 3sg.loc-over.there
Come on, we will talk to that one over there.
```

Q: What does {-burlu} mean?
A: ‘down there, over there’.

Q: What kind of word does {-burlu} attach to?
A: Pronouns.

Q: What does {nhungala-burlu} mean in 253/61?
A: ‘(to) that one over there’.

## Far away: galyi
id: galyi
grammar: Locations and directions › Locations, p. 65

{galyi} is a word on its own meaning ‘far away’:

```ex 388/4
yundru  muna-manhdha-rra nhinha       nhangala galyi
2sg.erg ?-get-future     3sg.masc.acc 3sg.loc  far
You will meet him over there, far away.
```

Q: What does {galyi} mean?
A: ‘far away’.

Q: Is {galyi} a word on its own or an ending?
A: A word on its own.

Q: What does 388/4 mean?
A: ‘You will meet him over there, far away.’

## Over there, far off: -gari
id: gari-far
grammar: Locations and directions › Locations, p. 65–66

Another way to say ‘far’ is the ending {-gari}. It is very like {galyi}, but it attaches to the end of a word (here, to pronouns). It often just means ‘there’, with the idea that it's far away.

```ex 58
nhani-gari        nhungka ngurra-lunga  gula-gala
3sg.nom-there.far 3sg.dat camp-locative sit-present
There in his camp he sits.
```

```ex 88/24
nadya-nga ngathu  maradhara-anha    nhungala-gari
see-past  1sg.erg possum-accusative 3sg.loc-there.far
I saw a possum up there.
```

In 58 the listener knows he is sitting far away, in his camp; in 88/24, the possum was up there, somewhere far away.

Q: How is {-gari} different from {galyi}?
A: {-gari} attaches to the end of a word; {galyi} is a word on its own.

Q: What does {-gari} often mean?
A: ‘there’, with the idea of far away.

Q: What does {nadya-nga ngathu maradhara-anha nhungala-gari} mean?
A: ‘I saw a possum up there.’

## Going a long way: gampa
id: gampa
grammar: Locations and directions › Locations, p. 66

The word {gampa} also means ‘far away’, often about physical distance, like going a long way.

```ex 160/32
wikarla  ngali   gampa yanhtha-ba-rra
tomorrow 1dl.nom far   go-away-future
Tomorrow the two of us will go a long way.
```

```ex 182/54
gampa nganyi  yanhtha-gala
far   1sg.nom go-present
I'm going far away.
```

{gampa} is often used together with {-gari}:

```ex 78/14
nhani-gari        dhithi-ani     gampa dhali-ani
3sg.nom-there.far dog-nominative far   good-nominative
That dog (far away) is good.
```

With {walya} ‘not’, it means ‘not far’: {nhani-gari walya gampa} ‘it is over there, not far away’.

Q: How do you say ‘I'm going far away’?
A: {gampa nganyi yanhtha-gala}.

Q: How do you say ‘not far’?
A: {walya gampa}.

Q: Which ending is often used together with {gampa}?
A: {-gari}.

## Close by: wina
id: wina
grammar: Locations and directions › Locations, p. 66–67

The word for ‘close by’ is {wina}. Here it is used to talk about sitting close to the fire:

```ex 149/21
wina  ngali   barnrdi-rra  wina-warra      ngali   gulha-rra  wiyi-lya
close 1dl.nom cross-future close-belonging 1dl.nom sit-future fire-there
We will sit down close to the fire.
lit: We will move close to sit close to the fire.
```

Q: What does {wina} mean?
A: ‘close, close by’.

Q: Which words mean ‘far’, and which means ‘close’?
A: {galyi}, {gampa} and {-gari} mean ‘far’; {wina} means ‘close’.

Q: What does 149/21 mean?
A: ‘We will sit down close to the fire.’

## Going away or coming back: -dhika
id: dhika
grammar: Locations and directions › Directions › -dhika on verbs, p. 67–68

The ending {-dhika} on a verb shows that **going away** or **coming back** is involved: going away from the speaker towards something else, or away from some other place towards the speaker.

```ex 120/56
gulha-dhika-wa     galka-rra     ngali-ngu    yanggu-andru
sit-away-emphatic strike-future 1dl-ergative spear-instrumental
Don't go too close, the two of us will kill it with a spear.
```

The speaker tells the listener to move away from the thing they are talking about.

```ex 198/6
ngathu  inha    ngutya-dhika-rra ingka   nguthi
1sg.erg 2sg.acc give-back-future 2sg.dat meat
I give back your meat to you.
```

The meat goes away from the speaker, back to the listener.

```ex 262/6
manhdha-dhika-rra      nhanha-guru   bakaranyi
grasp-back-imperative 3sg.acc-there boomerang
Bring that boomerang here.
```

The boomerang comes away from where it is, towards the speaker.

Q: What does {-dhika} add to {ngutya} ‘give’?
A: Giving back: {ngutya-dhika}.

Q: How do you say ‘bring’ (from {manhdha} ‘get, grasp’)?
A: {manhdha-dhika}.

Q: What two directions can {-dhika} show?
A: Going away from the speaker, or coming back towards the speaker.

## Going round and round: -dhika-dhika
id: dhikadhika
grammar: Locations and directions › Directions › -dhikadhika on verbs, p. 68

With {-dhika} twice in a row, the meaning changes from ‘going away’ to **going around in circles**.

```ex 95/31
ngarnidya-nga nganytya nhani-guru    dhithi-ani     guthi-dhika-dhika-gala
father-dative 1sg.dat  3sg.nom-there dog-nominative run-back-back-present
My father's dog is running around.
```

The dog is running round and round in circles.

```ex 405/21
nunpa-dhika-dhika nhanha-guru   yandra-anha
push-back-back    3sg.acc-there rock-accusative
Roll that stone.
```

The stone turns round and round.

Q: What does {-dhika-dhika} mean?
A: Going around in circles.

Q: What does {guthi-dhika-dhika-gala} mean?
A: ‘running around’.

Q: How do you say ‘roll (it)’, from {nunpa} ‘push’?
A: {nunpa-dhika-dhika}.

## Up and down: -warri
id: warri
grammar: Locations and directions › Directions › -warri on verbs, p. 68–69

The ending {-warri} on a verb shows that the movement goes **up or down**, above or below the speaker.

```ex 529/15
manhdha-warri nhanha-guru   yanggu-anha      / gatyi-li-ba-langu
get-vertical  3sg.acc-there spear-accusative / break-apart-motion-possibility
Pick up that spear. (It) might break.
```

The spear is below the speaker, on the ground, and has to be picked up.

```ex 408/24
yandra nhani-guru    buli-warri-rra       mirgala-lunga  ngaka-anga
rock   3sg.nom-there fall-vertical-future slope-locative water-dative
This stone will fall down along the steep bank into the water.
```

As the stone falls, it moves further and further down until it lands in the river.

Q: What does {-warri} show?
A: That the movement goes up or down (above or below the speaker).

Q: How do you say ‘pick up’ (from {manhdha} ‘get’)?
A: {manhdha-warri}.

Q: In 408/24, which word means ‘into the water’?
A: {ngaka-anga}.

## Away from the speaker: -ba
id: ba
grammar: Locations and directions › Directions › -ba on verbs, p. 69

The ending {-ba} on a verb means the action moves **away from the speaker**.

```ex 196/4
mura walga-ani       buka yanhtha-ba-rra giga-anha            manhdha-rra
OK   woman-nominative all  go-away-future bullrushes-accusative get-future
Now all of you women go out to fetch bullrushes.
```

The women will go away from the speaker to look for bullrushes.

```ex 459/11
ga  gulha-lha   nganyi  nangala / ini     yanhtha-ba
yes sit-factive 1sg.nom 3sg.loc / 2sg.nom go-away
Yes, I stop here, you go on.
```

The speaker and the listener were walking together; now the listener goes on, away from the speaker.

Q: What does {-ba} add to {yanhtha} ‘go’?
A: Going away from the speaker: {yanhtha-ba} ‘go away, go on’.

Q: What does {ini yanhtha-ba} mean?
A: ‘You go on.’

Q: Name the four direction endings on verbs in this unit.
A: {-dhika} (away or back), {-dhika-dhika} (around), {-warri} (up or down) and {-ba} (away from the speaker).
