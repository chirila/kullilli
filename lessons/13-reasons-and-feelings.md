# Reasons and feelings

Saying *because* and *that's why*, and talking about how you feel.

## Because: the ablative
id: because
grammar: Next steps › Reasons, p. 74

You already know how to ask ‘why?’ with {mina-anga}. To answer, Kullilli can use the **ablative** case. The ablative usually shows movement away from something, but it can also mean ‘because of’.

```ex 292/36
galka-iyi-nga          nganyi  nhungurra-guru dhina-anha     buli-nga  nganyi
strike-reflexive-past 1sg.nom 3sg.abl-there  foot-accusative fall-past 1sg.nom
I knocked my foot because of it and fell down.
```

The speaker fell because they knocked their foot on something: the ablative pronoun {nhungurra-guru} gives the reason.

Q: Which case can mean ‘because of’?
A: The ablative.

Q: In 292/36, why did the speaker fall down?
A: They knocked their foot on something.

Q: Which word in 292/36 means ‘because of it’?
A: {nhungurra-guru}.

## That's why: yala-anga
id: thats-why
grammar: Next steps › Reasons, p. 74

Another way to talk about reasons is {yala-anga} ‘that's why’. It looks like {mina-anga} ‘why?’: both have the dative ending {-anga}. {yala} adds the meaning ‘that’, so {yala-anga} is ‘(for) that (reason)’.

```ex 233/41
yala-anga   nhani-guru    ingka   malyu warlpa-gala
that-dative 3sg.nom-there 2sg.dat anger rise-present
That's why he is angry with you.
```

Q: What does {yala-anga} mean?
A: ‘That's why.’

Q: Which question word has the same ending as {yala-anga}?
A: {mina-anga} ‘why?’

Q: What does {yala-anga nhani-guru ingka malyu warlpa-gala} mean?
A: ‘That's why he is angry with you.’

## How I feel: balhthi-iyi-gala
id: i-feel
grammar: Next steps › Feelings, p. 75

The sources have quite few words for feelings. That doesn't mean Kullilli people didn't have them, just that not many were written down. Here are the words and phrases we know.

{balhthi-iyi-gala} means ‘I feel’. Word by word, {balhthi} means ‘fall, come down’, {-iyi} means ‘self’, and {-gala} means it is happening now. Combine it with words like these:

```words
yawura | hot
dhilaba | cold
dhali | good
mipa | bad
```

```ex
dhali balhthi-iyi-gala  nganyi
good  feel-self-present I.nom
I feel good.
```

```ex
yawura balhthi-iyi-gala  nganyi
hot    feel-self-present I.nom
I feel hot.
```

Q: Which three parts make up {balhthi-iyi-gala}?
A: {balhthi} ‘fall, come down’, {-iyi} ‘self’ and {-gala} (now).

Q: How do you say ‘I feel cold’?
A: {dhilaba balhthi-iyi-gala nganyi}.

Q: What does {mipa} mean?
A: ‘bad’.

## Feeling sick: munka-iyi-gala
id: feel-sick
grammar: Next steps › Feelings, p. 75

{munka} means ‘feel sick’. Like {balhthi-iyi-gala}, it takes {-iyi} ‘self’ and {-gala} ‘now’.

```ex
munka-iyi-gala          nganyi
feel.sick-self-present I.nom
I feel sick.
```

If you have a headache, use {munka-iyi-gala} with {guka} ‘head’ to say your head hurts:

```ex
guka nganytya munka-iyi-gala
head my       feel.sick-self-present
My head is hurting.
```

Q: What does {munka} mean?
A: ‘feel sick’.

Q: How do you say ‘My head is hurting’?
A: {guka nganytya munka-iyi-gala}.

Q: What is the word for ‘head’?
A: {guka}.

## Crying and being angry
id: cry-angry
grammar: Next steps › Feelings, p. 75–76

{ganguri} is a verb meaning ‘cry’. Just add a verb ending such as {-gala}, {-nga}, {-gali} or {-rra}. The first sentence is from Charlie Phillips; the second was made up by the guide's authors.

```ex
ganguri-gala nhaniguru warliwani
cry-present  that      child
That child is crying.
```

```ex made-up
ganguri-rra nhaniguru warliwani
cry-future  that      child
That child will cry.
```

{malyu} means ‘anger’. It is always used with the verb {warlpa} ‘rise’, so {malyu warlpa-gala} literally means ‘anger rises’ and is used to say someone is angry.

```ex
malyu warlpa-gala  nhaniyi garnaani
anger rise-present this    man
This man is angry.
```

Q: How do you say ‘That child will cry’?
A: {ganguri-rra nhaniguru warliwani}.

Q: What does {malyu warlpa-gala} mean literally, and what does it mean?
A: Literally ‘anger rises’; it means someone is angry.

Q: Which verb is always used with {malyu}?
A: {warlpa} ‘rise’.
