# Saying no

Saying ‘not’ and ‘no’, ‘without’, and ‘unable’.

## Not: walya
id: not-walya
grammar: Walya ‘not, no’ › Examples of ‘not’, p. 90

{walya} is the word for ‘not’ and ‘no’. It usually comes at the beginning of the sentence.

```ex 46
walya murta-gala nganyi
not   hurt-present 1sg.nom
I am not sick.
```

```ex 170/42
walya girni-ani       nhani
not   heavy-nominative 3sg.nom
This is not heavy.
```

```ex 102/38
walya nhani-guru    ingka   bakaranyi-ani
not   3sg.nom-there 2sg.dat boomerang-nominative
This is not your boomerang.
```

```ex 91/27
walya ngathu  inha    nadya-gala  / yuru walbiri-anha
not   1sg.erg 2sg.acc see-present / only shade-accusative
I don't see you – only your shadow.
```

Q: What does {walya} mean?
A: ‘not’ or ‘no’.

Q: How do you say ‘I am not sick’?
A: {walya murta-gala nganyi}.

Q: What does {walya girni-ani nhani} mean?
A: ‘This is not heavy.’

## Without: -munhdhu
id: without
grammar: Walya ‘not, no’ › Privative suffix, p. 91

The ending {-munhdhu} on a noun means ‘without’: that thing is missing from the situation.

```ex 75/11
nguthi-munhdhu nganyi
meat-without   1sg.nom
I am without meat.
```

The speaker has no meat at the moment.

```ex 508/60 part
mura     mara-ni         nganytya dhaka-munhdhu
finished hand-nominative 1sg.dat  dirt-without
Already my hand is clean.
lit: My hand is without dirt.
```

The speaker has washed their hand, so it is ‘without dirt’.

Q: What does {-munhdhu} mean?
A: ‘without’.

Q: How do you say ‘I am without meat’?
A: {nguthi-munhdhu nganyi}.

Q: What does {dhaka-munhdhu} mean?
A: ‘without dirt’ (clean).

## There's no…: walya and -munhdhu
id: there-is-no
grammar: Walya ‘not, no’ › Walyu in the sense of ‘no’, p. 90

To say there is none of something (*There's no water*), use {walya} together with {-munhdhu} ‘without’ on the noun. We aren't sure yet whether {walya} is needed every time.

```ex 26
walya ngaka-munhdhu
not   water-without
No water here.
```

```ex 15
walya yanhdha wiyi-anha          manhdha-rra yundru  / walya wiyi-munhdhu
not   go      firewood-accusative get-future 2sg.erg / not   fire-without
Go get wood, there's no fire.
```

Q: How do you say ‘No water here’?
A: {walya ngaka-munhdhu}.

Q: Which two words or endings are used to say ‘there's no…’?
A: {walya} ‘not’ and the ending {-munhdhu} ‘without’.

Q: What does {walya wiyi-munhdhu} mean?
A: ‘There's no fire.’

## Unable: barninyi
id: unable
grammar: Walya ‘not, no’, p. 91

Another way to say ‘no’ is to say that something **can't** happen. The word {barninyi} means ‘unable’. It often comes with the ending {-dhali} on the verb, which seems to mean that someone is unable to do something in this particular situation.

```ex 298/42
barninyi nganyi  wira-dhali-nga  nhungka
unable   1sg.nom wait-ability-past 3sg.dat
I can't wait for it.
```

```ex 300/44
barninyi ngathu  nadya-dhali-nga
unable   1sg.erg see-ability-past
I could not see it.
```

You have seen {barninyi} before, in ‘I couldn't find his boomerang’. We don't know yet exactly how saying ‘can't’ with {barninyi} differs from using {walya} ‘not’ with a verb.

Q: What does {barninyi} mean?
A: ‘unable’.

Q: Which ending often goes on the verb with {barninyi}?
A: {-dhali}.

Q: What does {barninyi ngathu nadya-dhali-nga} mean?
A: ‘I could not see it.’
